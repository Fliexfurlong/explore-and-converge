# Compare and converge

Use when different routes need a choice or a leading option needs checking. Do not force this stage when the user wants divergence only.

## Check comparability

Group and deduplicate by mechanism while preserving meaningful differences. Check whether candidates serve the same goal. Explain differing goals rather than forcing one ranking. Set aside confirmed hard-constraint failures and retain unknown feasibility for verification. Preferences can be traded off; exploratory settings must be checked against reality again.

## Check relationships before deciding how to converge

Make a light check when relationships could change the conclusion. With many candidates, group by mechanism or level and inspect only relationships that affect judgment. Do not require a complete relationship matrix.

| Relationship | Appropriate response | Check |
|---|---|---|
| Genuinely exclusive | Compare tradeoffs, choose, or defer | Is there a real conflict, or was the question merely phrased as either/or? |
| Complementary | Assess a combination | Added cost, coupling, burden, and negative interactions. Individually attractive options do not justify selecting everything. |
| Dependent | State prerequisites and sequence | Are prerequisites satisfied, and is the order actually necessary? |
| Different applicability conditions | Retain each with a conditional role | Name an observable condition that changes the choice; do not stop at vague "it depends." |
| Different levels | Separate goal, strategy, and specific action before organizing them | Do not treat an overall strategy and one action within it as equivalent candidates. |
| Unclear | Preserve the unknown relationship and verify the decisive condition | Do not force a label or single ranking. |

Relationships can overlap: sequentially dependent options may also complement each other. Complementary options may still compete for this round's budget, time, or shared resource. Distinguish retained value from implementation priority; deferral is not rejection.

Explain how components jointly serve the goal and when combining them is worthwhile. A position marker and a question note might restore location and reasoning respectively, but the combination may lose value if recording effort exceeds the user's tolerance. Reassess combinations rather than adding individual effects or scores.

## Choose a method for the decision difficulty

| Method | When useful | Output |
|---|---|---|
| PCI: pros, cons, interesting points | Quick understanding of a few directions | Decisive benefits, drawbacks, and discoveries whose value is still uncertain. |
| Benefit / effort | Prioritization | Supported benefit and effort estimates, including ranges where appropriate, rather than bare high/low labels. |
| NUF: novel, useful, feasible | Initial screening of creative candidates | Keep the three dimensions separate, allow insufficient evidence, and do not compensate for hard-constraint violations with novelty. |
| Two-option comparison | Only two genuine candidates remain | Differences most likely to change the choice and conditions for each. |
| Weighted matrix | Multiple criteria justify quantification and inputs are explainable | Calculate using the quantitative reference; keep subjective weights and scores visible. |
| Six Thinking Hats / SWOT | A few mature directions need multiple perspectives | Select views that fill blind spots without filling a template for every early idea. |
| Counterargument check | A leading candidate needs a blind-spot check | The strongest objection, its supporting evidence, and whether it changes the choice. |
| Premortem | Failure paths are the key uncertainty | Plausible failure mechanisms, warning signs, mitigations, and abandonment conditions. |
| MoSCoW | A direction exists and this round's scope needs stating | Must, should, could, and won't this round; deferred items may include observable reopening conditions. |
| Action / clear statement | A recommendation needs validation or communication | A one-sentence recommendation and the most informative next step; add owners and deadlines only when relevant. |

## Choose the right result

The result may be a choice, combination, conditional allocation, or sequence; alternatives or deferral may also be appropriate. Do not require all four result forms or add steps for completeness. Design validation when missing facts determine the winner. Reframe when a shared premise is flawed. Counterargument checking is not opposition to everything and should not invent reasons that make every direction impossible.

If facts stay the same, changing presentation order, emphasis, or unverified endorsements should not change utility or cost judgments. User preferences are legitimate decision inputs and may change a recommendation. Explain why without presenting preference as effectiveness evidence. Reorganize candidates and relationships when goals, real constraints, or evidence actually change; do not become rigid for the appearance of consistency.

Explain tradeoffs and separate potential, feasibility, and evidence maturity. A recommendation does not authorize implementation. Do not automatically invoke development skills, write specifications, commit code, or execute a candidate.
