# Reframe the question

Read when the framing, premises, or goal itself limits possible directions. Use existing material first; do not turn every exploration into a requirements interview.

## Goal, form, and constraints

Separate "we need a particular thing" into "what needs to be achieved" and "how the current form serves it." A check-in feature might serve continuity in learning; reminders, lower startup effort, and recovery after an interruption use different mechanisms. The inferred goal still needs support from context; do not decide it for the user.

Identify premises that could change the answer. Distinguish facts, inferences, preferences, and exploratory settings. Check the source of hard constraints; a previously tentative solution does not automatically become a hard constraint now.

## Choose one useful action

| Method | Gap it addresses | Action and output |
|---|---|---|
| Question expansion / 5W1H | Unclear what is missing | Select useful dimensions of who, what, where, when, why, and how. Produce questions that could change the route without filling every category. |
| Concept fan | Every answer assumes one form | Ask what the proposed solution is a way of achieving. Move up far enough to open alternatives, then return to concrete mechanisms. Step back if abstraction loses the goal. |
| First principles | A conventional practice looks mandatory | Separate supported conditions from convention and recombine ways to achieve the goal. Basic facts need evidence, not just plausibility. |
| Causal inquiry | Symptoms and bottlenecks are confused | Explore possible causes while retaining competing explanations. Distinguish them with evidence; do not ask why five times mechanically or mistake a coherent story for a root cause. |
| Research existing approaches | Precedents could change judgment | Check a few representative approaches to the current bottleneck. Retain sources, mechanisms, and limits. A suitable conventional solution may be enough to finish. |

## Handoff

Once the question is clear, give the more precise framing and explain how it changes the choice space. Diverge if candidates are needed; compare directly if they already exist. Do not continue abstracting just to complete a process.
