# Develop an idea

Use for a promising but incomplete idea. Do not require the user to supply complete requirements first.

## Find the missing part

| State | Useful action | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete idea; what comes next? | Adjacent possibilities | Capabilities or settings that success could enable, without expanding the current scope. |
| Missing its other half | Combine with another domain, old material, a constraint, or an inversion | Which combination changes the mechanism and which is decorative. |
| Vague intuition | State the seed and identify gaps | A clear sentence plus unknown mechanisms, settings, or examples; the seed may remain tentative. |
| Tried and failed | Learn from the failure | Separate mechanism, execution, and environmental causes; identify revisions supported by evidence. |
| Works in its original use; could it transfer? | Try a transfer | What remains, what changes, and where it fails in the new setting. |
| Needs to become a viable candidate | Light stress test | Key assumptions, a minimal example, failure conditions, and a validation action. |

Completeness is not the goal in itself. Fill the link that most changes understanding first. Move to validation when evidence is missing. Diverge if every extension repeats the same mechanism, and return when a new seed deserves development.

Do not preserve a clearly mistaken direction because of prior investment. Do not treat a potentially valuable direction as irrelevant merely because evidence is temporarily absent. Retaining potential and marking insufficient evidence can coexist.
